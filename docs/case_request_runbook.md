# Runbook - handling a customer fraud request

For a Fraud Analyst in a threat research team. Example used throughout: ticket T07 (SIMULATED customer C03).

## 1. First hour: qualify the request
| Question to ask the customer | Why |
|---|---|
| What exactly did you see, since when, on which flow (login, sign-up, checkout)? | sets the time window and the endpoint |
| How many users complained, and can you share 3-5 example timestamps (no personal data)? | anchors the search |
| Did anything change on your side: app release, SDK version, campaign, new rule? | rules out data issues (E5) and campaigns (E8) |
| What did your application do with our recommendations (allow / challenge / deny)? | the customer keeps the final decision |
| Who must get the answer, and by when? | sets the report format |

Priority: **high** if accounts are taken over or money is at stake (answer within 1 business day); **medium** for a KPI
question (3 days); **low** for a request for data (5 days).

## 2. Extract the data (queries in `sql/` and `docs/kibana_es_queries.md`)
1. Customer KPIs per week and their robust z-score (`out_monitoring`, `out_alerts`) - is the customer already flagged?
2. Data checks first: duplicates and empty fields per SDK version (`out_sdk_quality`). A broken SDK can look like an attack, or hide one.
3. Calendar check: declared campaigns (`customer_calendar`) - a volume spike with normal quality metrics is not an attack.
4. Pattern queries: device clusters (Q1), success then credential change (Q2), sign-ups per device (Q3), crawler verification (Q4).

## 3. Analyse to the cause
- Find the **entity that ties the events together** (device hash, ASN, user-agent version, e-mail domain), not only the IP.
- Count what the detection **recommended** for those events, and what the customer **did**.
- Separate: credentials validated (exposed accounts) and accounts taken over (an action followed).
- Write down what the data cannot say (source of a credential leak, delayed fraud reports).

## 4. Answer (template: `docs/customer_case_report_T07.md`, generated by `python src/case_report.py --ticket T07`)
Five-line summary, timeline, affected accounts **as counts**, what detection saw and missed, actions with an owner and a date,
what remains uncertain. Plain language; no internal rule names unless the customer knows them.

## 5. When to call a Solution Engineer
- The fix needs a configuration change on the customer side (rule, integration, SDK).
- The customer questions detection performance (precision / recall, false positives on their users).
- The request is commercial (new endpoint to protect, new product).
Share: the case report, the KPI gap, and the expected effect of the proposed change.

## 6. Close the loop
- Add or update the card in `docs/fraud_pattern_library.md`.
- Log any data problem in `docs/data_issues_log.md`.
- Propose the signal (`docs/detection_signal_proposals.md`) with its offline measure.
- Check the KPI two weeks later and tell the customer.
